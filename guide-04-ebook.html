01

Bismillah — The Opening

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful

Every New Year, millions of people write goals. By February most of them are forgotten. Maybe you have felt it too: a strong wish to change, followed by a slow return to the old habits.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that real change starts in a place no calendar can reach: the heart. When the heart is turned to Allah, discipline stops being a burden and becomes a joy. This guide will show you how, insha'Allah.

02

The Problem

Many Muslims struggle with a gap between what they believe and how they live. They want to pray on time, speak gently, work hard, give charity and avoid sins. But busy schedules, weak habits and bad company pull them back. Then guilt arrives, and guilt without a plan only produces more guilt.

Self-help books offer motivation, but motivation fades. Islam offers something deeper: taqwa (God-consciousness), daily accountability, small steady deeds and a community that pulls you upward. This is a complete system for character change.

Signs You Are Facing This

  • You start good habits with energy but quit within days.
  • You lose your temper, then regret it, then do it again.
  • Your time disappears on your phone and you do not know where it went.
  • You feel distant from Allah but do not know how to return.
  • You have goals, but they are not connected to your purpose in life.
03

The Islamic Answer

The Quran and Sunnah give us the pillars of change: intention, taqwa, planning for tomorrow and constant return to Allah.

04

The Explanation

Taqwa: The Engine of Lasting Change

Taqwa means living with awareness that Allah sees you and that you will meet Him. It is the quality that Allah says makes a person most honourable. It works in private as well as in public, which is why it changes habits in a way that rules from outside cannot.

The Prophet ﷺ gave a simple three-part formula in one hadith: keep taqwa wherever you are, follow every mistake with a good deed and treat people with good character. You do not need to be perfect. You need to return quickly. A fall followed by repentance and a good deed is a step forward, not a defeat.

Key takeawayDo not wait to be perfect. Keep returning, and keep adding good after every mistake.
إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ
Inna akramakum 'indal-lahi atqakum
“Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you.”
Surah Al-Hujurat 49:13

Niyyah and Goals: Plan for Tomorrow with Allah

The Quran tells every soul to look at what it has sent forward for tomorrow. That is Islamic goal-setting: planning with the Akhirah in mind. Begin with niyyah. Ask: why do I want this goal? If the answer is to please Allah and benefit people, your goal becomes worship.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the strong believer is better and more beloved to Allah than the weak believer, while in both there is good. Strive for what benefits you, ask Allah for help and do not give up. If something goes wrong, do not say “if only,” but say “Allah decreed and what He willed, He did.”

Pick a few goals in four areas: worship, character, knowledge and service. Make each goal specific and small, such as “pray Fajr on time five days a week.” Make Istikhara for big decisions, then act with trust.

Key takeawayA goal without niyyah is a wish. A goal with niyyah is worship.

“The strong believer is better and more beloved to Allah than the weak believer, though in both there is good. Be keen on what benefits you, seek help from Allah and do not feel helpless.”

Sahih Muslim 2664

Discipline: Small Deeds, Done Daily

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most regular, even if small. A short daily habit beats a big burst of effort that dies in a week. Start with a five-minute routine and protect it.

Time is the raw material of discipline. The Prophet ﷺ said there are two blessings that many people lose: health and free time. Decide the three most important things in your day and do them first. Reduce what steals your hours, especially endless scrolling.

Good company is part of discipline. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a person follows the way of his close friend, so choose carefully who you walk with. Surround yourself with people who remind you of Allah and sharpen your goals.

Key takeawayConsistency, not intensity, builds character.

“The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done regularly, even if they are small.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6464, Sahih Muslim 783

“There are two blessings that many people lose: health and free time.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6412

“A man is on the religion of his close friend, so let each of you look at whom he takes as a friend.”

Sunan Abu Dawud 4833 (Hasan)

Muhasabah: Hold Yourself to Account Every Day

Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) is reported to have said: hold yourselves to account before you are held to account. Muhasabah is a short daily review before sleep. Ask: what did I do for Allah today? Where did I fall short? What will I do better tomorrow?

Keep it gentle but honest. Thank Allah for the good, seek forgiveness for the mistakes and write one small improvement for tomorrow. This simple habit, done for a few minutes, trains the heart to stay awake.

Key takeawayFive honest minutes at night can change the next day.
Scholarly note. Umar's saying is quoted by Imam at-Tirmidhi and is widely cited by scholars as the foundation of muhasabah, the daily self-accounting of the believer.

Purify the Heart: Anger, Pride and Envy

Real character shows when you are tested. The Prophet ﷺ said the strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling but the one who controls himself in anger. When someone angers you, pause, say “A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim,” change your position and delay your reply.

Pride is a heavy disease. The Prophet ﷺ warned that no one with even an atom's weight of arrogance in his heart will enter Paradise. The cure is to remember that everything you have is a gift from Allah, serve people humbly and accept correction with gratitude.

Envy is cured by du'a for the person you envy and by gratitude for what you have. Allah promised that if you are grateful, He will give you more.

Key takeawayA heart that is purified will make every other habit easier.

“The strong person is not the one who overcomes others by wrestling. The strong person is the one who controls himself when angry.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6114, Sahih Muslim 2609

“No one will enter Paradise who has in his heart the weight of an atom of arrogance.”

Sahih Muslim 91

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Setting ten big goals at once. Start with two or three.
  • Waiting for motivation instead of building a routine.
  • Hiding failure instead of repenting and restarting right away.
  • Comparing yourself with others instead of with your own yesterday.
  • Trying to change alone. Share your goal with a trusted friend.

Questions People Ask

What if I miss a day of my habit?Return the next day. Do not turn one missed day into a month of missed days. The Prophet ﷺ taught us to follow a bad deed with a good deed.
How many goals should I set?Two or three at a time is enough: one for worship, one for character and one for work or study.
Is it a sin to set worldly goals?No. With a good intention, work, study and family goals become worship.
05

Practical Application: Start Today

Character is built, not wished for. Here are seven daily steps that anyone can start today.

  1. Write your whyIn two sentences, write why you want to change and how it will please Allah.
  2. Choose three goalsOne for worship, one for character and one for work or learning. Make them small and specific.
  3. Fix a morning anchorPray Fajr on time and read your morning adhkar. Everything else in the day follows this.
  4. Protect one hourChoose one hour for focused work or learning with your phone away.
  5. Keep a good companionShare your goals with a friend who will encourage you and ask you each week.
  6. Do muhasabah each nightFive minutes before sleep: thank, review, seek forgiveness and write tomorrow's improvement.
  7. Give something dailyGive a smile, a kind word, a small amount of charity or an act of service. Make it a habit.

06

From My Leadership Journey

In 2005 I was chosen as the amir of the Muslim students at Dilla University. I was young and I learned fast that a title does not change a person, but character does. Students did not follow my position. They followed my honesty, my patience and the way I treated people when no one was watching.

Years later, in public leadership, that lesson was confirmed. Skills open doors, but character keeps them open. A leader with taqwa is trusted even when he is not present, and that is the leader this Ummah needs.

07

Reflection Questions

  1. Which habit would change my life the most if I did it daily for one year?
  2. When I am alone, am I the same person that I am in public?
  3. What distracts me most from my goals, and what will I do about it?
  4. Who in my life helps me to grow closer to Allah?
  5. What is one mistake I must stop repeating, and what good deed will I put after it?
08

Conclusion and Call to Action

Character change is a journey of a thousand small steps, each taken for Allah. You will stumble, and that is part of the path. What matters is that you rise, repent and continue.

Allah says that he succeeds who purifies his soul. Start with one habit tonight, and let your life become a quiet witness for Islam.

Your next stepWrite your three goals tonight, and ask one friend to check on your progress next week.
