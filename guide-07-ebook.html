Bismillah — The Opening

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful

A small boy once sat down to eat with the Prophet ﷺ, and his hand wandered all over the dish. The Prophet ﷺ did not shout. He did not shame him. He said, “Young man, say Bismillah, eat with your right hand and eat from what is in front of you.” The boy said later that this became his way of eating for the rest of his life.

That is the power of gentle, clear, loving guidance. In an age of phones, games and constant noise, the Muslim parent needs that same calm wisdom. This guide will help you lead your children with it, insha'Allah.

02

The Problem

Parents today face challenges that earlier generations did not. Children spend hours online, learn values from videos and influencers, and meet ideas at school that clash with Islam. Parents are tired and busy, and many respond with either strictness without warmth or freedom without direction.

Both extremes fail. Children raised by shouting learn to hide. Children raised without boundaries learn to drift. The Prophet ﷺ gave a third way: mercy, example, clear teaching, justice between children and constant du'a.

Signs You Are Facing This

  • Your child talks to a screen more than to you.
  • You mostly speak to your children when you are correcting them.
  • Your children know about video games and trends better than Quran and the Prophet's ﷺ stories.
  • You have different rules for different children without fairness.
  • You pray for your children rarely, or only when a problem appears.

04

The Explanation

You Are the Leader: Start with Yourself

Allah begins the verse of protection with the self: protect yourselves, then your families. Children read their parents like a book. They notice if you pray, if you lie, how you speak to your spouse and what you do on your phone. A parent who wants a praying child must first become a praying parent.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that on the Day of Judgement each shepherd will be asked about his flock. That sounds heavy, but it is also a mercy: Allah gave you the most powerful tool in the child's life, your example. Use it well.

Key takeawayThe strongest teaching your child receives is the one you never say aloud.

“The Prophet ﷺ said to a young boy: Say Bismillah, eat with your right hand and eat from what is near you.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 5376, Sahih Muslim 2022

Love First: Mercy Opens the Heart

The Prophet ﷺ kissed his grandson Hasan, and a man who saw it said he had ten children and had never kissed any of them. The Prophet ﷺ replied that whoever shows no mercy will not be shown mercy. He also prayed while carrying his granddaughter Umamah, putting her down when he prostrated and lifting her when he stood up.

Children who feel loved can accept correction. Children who feel unloved reject even the truth. Give daily affection, eye contact, listening time and words of love. Say it clearly: I love you for the sake of Allah, and I am proud of you.

Key takeawayConnection before correction. A child's heart opens through mercy.

“The Prophet ﷺ kissed Al-Hasan ibn Ali. Al-Aqra' said: I have ten children and I have never kissed any of them. The Prophet ﷺ looked at him and said: Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 5997, Sahih Muslim 2318

“The Prophet ﷺ used to pray while carrying Umamah, the daughter of Zaynab. When he prostrated he put her down, and when he stood he lifted her.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 516, Sahih Muslim 543

Teach Faith Step by Step

Luqman is the model of a wise teacher. He started with the greatest message, Tawhid, then moved to prayer, good deeds and patience. He used a loving address, “O my little son.” Begin with the love of Allah before the fear of punishment. Teach the names of Allah, stories of the Prophets, short surahs and simple duas.

The Prophet ﷺ gave a clear timeline for prayer: command your children to pray at seven years, and be firm about it at ten. At seven, make it an invitation with praise and small rewards. At ten, make it an expectation with gentle consistency. Take your children to the mosque, and let them see you give charity and make du'a.

Use the home as a school of faith: Quran heard at home, Bismillah before meals, salam on entering, bedtime adhkar and stories of the Companions.

Key takeawayFaith is caught before it is taught. Let it fill your home.

“Command your children to pray when they are seven years old, and discipline them for it when they are ten, and separate them in their beds.”

Sunan Abu Dawud 495 (Hasan Sahih)

Be Just and Gentle in Discipline

Justice between children is a command. When a Companion wanted to give a gift to only one of his sons, the Prophet ﷺ said: fear Allah and be just between your children. Favouritism plants jealousy that can last a lifetime. Treat each child as an individual, but with equal love and fairness.

Discipline is teaching, not revenge. Never correct in anger. Correct in private. Separate the child from the mistake: “What you did was wrong,” not “You are bad.” Give clear rules, explain the reason and apply consequences calmly. The Prophet ﷺ said that gentleness beautifies everything it touches.

Praise effort more than results. Say what you saw and name the quality: “I saw how patient you were with your sister. That is a quality Allah loves.”

Key takeawayFairness builds trust. Gentleness builds obedience that comes from love.

“Gentleness is not found in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed from anything except that it disfigures it.”

Sahih Muslim 2594

Guard Their Hearts in the Digital Age

Screens are now part of childhood. The aim is not to hide your children from the world but to prepare them for it. Set clear family rules: no phones at meals or in bedrooms at night, screen limits by age, shared devices in common areas for young children and filters on devices.

Talk, do not only restrict. Ask: what do you watch, who do you talk to and what do you feel online? Teach them what to do if they see something wrong or if someone makes them uncomfortable. Make sure they know they can come to you without fear of harsh punishment.

Companions matter. The Prophet ﷺ said a person follows the religion of his close friend. Help your children find good friends: invite their friends home, join community programmes and mosque youth groups. Fill their time with sports, reading, skills and service so that there is less room for emptiness.

Key takeawayDo not just take the phone away. Replace it with something worthy.

“A man is upon the religion of his close friend, so let each of you look at whom he befriends.”

Sunan Abu Dawud 4833 (Hasan)

Make Du'a Your Greatest Parenting Tool

The prophets made du'a for their children. Ibrahim (peace be upon him) asked Allah to make him and his offspring keepers of prayer. Zakariyya asked for righteous offspring even when he was old. Parenting is the work of your hands and the work of your du'a.

Make du'a for your children by name, daily, especially in sujood and in the last part of the night. The Prophet ﷺ taught that a righteous child who prays for his parents is among the deeds that continue after death. Raise such a child, and your reward continues.

Key takeawayYou cannot control their choices, but you can ask the One who controls hearts.

“When a person dies, his deeds end except for three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him.”

Sahih Muslim 1631

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Correcting your child in front of others.
  • Comparing one child with another.
  • Making Islam feel like a list of prohibitions without love.
  • Giving a phone or tablet to keep a child quiet.
  • Expecting children to practise what their parents do not.

Questions People Ask

My teenager is distant. What can I do?Spend time doing things they enjoy, listen more than you speak, avoid lectures and make du'a. Keep the door open and show consistent love.
How strict should I be about prayer at ten?Be firm but gentle. Make it a family habit, pray together and ask them to help you build a routine. Anger usually pushes children away.
What is a healthy screen limit?There is no single number. Set limits by age, keep screens out of bedrooms at night and balance them with outdoor play, reading and family time.
05

Practical Application: Start Today

You do not need to be a perfect parent. You need to be a present, consistent and prayerful one. Start with these seven steps.

  1. Fix yourself firstChoose one habit you want your children to copy and practise it openly, such as praying on time or reading Quran.
  2. Give each child ten minutes alone with youDaily if possible. No phones, no lectures. Listen and enjoy their company.
  3. Create a family routine of faithSalam on entering, Bismillah before eating, bedtime adhkar and one story from the Sirah each week.
  4. Set family digital rulesAgree on screen times and places together, and follow the same rules yourself.
  5. Praise specificallyEach day, notice one good quality or effort and name it.
  6. Practise fairnessCheck your gifts, time and attention to make sure each child feels equally loved.
  7. Make du'a for each childEach day, ask Allah by name for each child's guidance, health and righteousness.

06

From My Leadership Journey

In my work as a public leader I learned that leadership is not shouting orders. It is setting direction, modelling the standard, listening to people and forgiving mistakes while keeping the goal clear. These are exactly the habits good parents need.

I also learned that respect cannot be forced. Whether it was staff, community members or my own family, people follow those who are consistent and fair, and who show care for them. Parenting is the first and most important leadership role any of us will ever have.

07

Reflection Questions

  1. What do my children learn about Islam from watching me, not from listening to me?
  2. When did I last listen to my child without correcting or lecturing?
  3. Do my children feel equally loved and treated fairly?
  4. What does my child's digital life look like, and how do I know?
  5. Who is praying for my children by name, and is it me?
08

Conclusion and Call to Action

Raising children is the longest project of your life, and its reward is the greatest. A child raised with love, faith and example becomes a source of light for you now and after you are gone.

Start with one step this week. Put your phone away, kneel down, look your child in the eye and say, “I love you for the sake of Allah.” That moment may change more than a thousand lectures.

Your next stepChoose one child and spend ten undistracted minutes with them today. Then begin your daily du'a for them.
