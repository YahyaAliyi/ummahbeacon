Bismillah — The Opening

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful

Remember your last Ramadan. The first nights were full of energy, the mosque was crowded and the Quran felt close. Then the days passed, work returned and by the last ten nights you were tired. Then Eid arrived, and a month later the habits had gone.

Does it have to be that way? Allah told us the purpose of fasting: that you may attain taqwa. A Ramadan that changes you is possible, and it begins with planning. This guide will help you build it, insha'Allah.

02

The Problem

Many Muslims treat Ramadan as a season of tradition: long iftar tables, late nights and a rush to complete the Quran. They finish with tired bodies but little change in character, prayer or discipline. Others start strongly and burn out by the middle of the month.

The Prophet ﷺ gave us a model that is strong yet balanced: more Quran, more night prayer, more charity, more sincerity and special attention to the last ten nights. When Ramadan is planned this way, it changes the year that follows.

Signs You Are Facing This

  • You do not have a clear plan for Ramadan before it begins.
  • You focus on food more than on worship.
  • Your Quran reading drops after the first week.
  • You lose your temper or waste hours on screens while fasting.
  • Your good habits vanish a few weeks after Eid.
03

04

The Explanation

Prepare Before Ramadan Arrives

The Prophet ﷺ increased his fasting in Sha'ban, preparing the body and the heart. Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) said that she never saw him fast a whole month except Ramadan, and she never saw him fast more in any month than in Sha'ban. Use the weeks before Ramadan for soft training: increase Quran, fast some days, make up missed fasts and settle disputes.

Write your plan before the first night. Choose a few specific goals in four areas: Quran, prayer, charity and character. For example: finish the Quran once, pray tarawih in congregation, give a fixed amount daily and stop one bad habit. Tell a trusted friend or your family so that you can encourage each other.

Key takeawayA planned Ramadan is much stronger than a hopeful Ramadan.

“Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) said: I never saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ fast a complete month except Ramadan, and I did not see him fast more in any month than in Sha'ban.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 1969, Sahih Muslim 1156

Fast with Taqwa: Fast the Eyes, the Tongue and the Heart

The fast is more than hunger. Allah gave its goal: taqwa. A fasting person guards the tongue from backbiting and lies, the eyes from what is forbidden and the heart from envy and anger. If someone insults you, say, “I am fasting.” The Prophet ﷺ taught that fasting is a shield.

Keep your meals simple. Suhoor is blessed, and the Prophet ﷺ encouraged it. Break your fast with dates and water, and make du'a. Avoid heavy overeating that makes you too tired for the night prayer.

Use your day well. Schedule your hardest work early, leave a short time for Quran after Dhuhr and Asr, and keep the screens limited. Treat each day of Ramadan like a precious gift, because it is.

Key takeawayHunger alone is not fasting. Fasting is hunger with taqwa.

“Eat suhoor, for in suhoor there is blessing.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 1923, Sahih Muslim 1095

“The thirst has gone, the veins are moist and the reward is confirmed, if Allah wills.”

Sunan Abu Dawud 2357 (Hasan)

The Month of the Quran: A Simple Reading Plan

Ramadan is the month in which the Quran was revealed. The Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was most generous in Ramadan when Jibril met him every night to review the Quran with him. Make the Quran the centre of your month.

A simple plan: the Quran has 30 parts (juz), so reading one part a day completes it. A part is about 20 pages, which is four pages after each of the five daily prayers. Choose a translation or tafsir you trust and read a small portion with reflection each day. Even if you do not finish the whole Quran, a steady, understood reading is better than a quick reading without presence.

Key takeawayFour pages after each prayer completes the Quran in a month.

“The Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was most generous in Ramadan when Jibril met him. Jibril used to meet him every night in Ramadan and review the Quran with him.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6, Sahih Muslim 2308

Night Prayer, Du'a and Charity

The Prophet ﷺ promised forgiveness for whoever stands in the night prayer of Ramadan with faith and hope for reward. Pray tarawih with the congregation until the imam finishes, and if you can, add a few rak'ahs of qiyam in the last third of the night, when Allah descends and answers du'a.

Du'a is especially beloved in Ramadan. Make a list of your needs, your family's needs and the needs of the Ummah. Ask for forgiveness, guidance and ease.

Give as much as you can. Feeding a fasting person earns you a reward equal to his, without reducing his reward at all. Pay your Zakat in Ramadan if it is due, and prepare Zakat al-Fitr before Eid.

Key takeawayThe best Ramadan nights are the ones spent between sujood, Quran and tears of hope.

“Whoever stands in prayer during Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward, his previous sins will be forgiven.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 37, Sahih Muslim 759

“Whoever feeds a fasting person will have a reward like his, without reducing the reward of the fasting person at all.”

Sunan at-Tirmidhi 807 (Hasan Sahih)

The Last Ten Nights and Laylat al-Qadr

The Prophet ﷺ gave special attention to the last ten nights. Aisha reported that when the last ten began, he would tighten his waist wrapper, stay awake at night and wake his family. He also practised i'tikaf, secluding himself in the mosque for worship.

Laylat al-Qadr is in the last ten nights, and the odd nights are the most hoped for. Do not focus on guessing the date. Instead, strive in all ten nights. The Prophet ﷺ taught Aisha what to say if she met this night: “O Allah, You are the Pardoner and You love to pardon, so pardon me.”

Key takeawayOne night can be better than a thousand months. Do not miss it by waiting for a sign.

“When the last ten nights began, the Prophet ﷺ would tighten his waist wrapper, spend the night in worship and wake his family. He also used to perform i'tikaf in the last ten days until Allah took his soul.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 2024-2026, Sahih Muslim 1174

“Seek Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 2017

“Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and seeking reward, his previous sins will be forgiven.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 2014, Sahih Muslim 760
اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي
Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni
“O Allah, You are the Pardoner, You love to pardon, so pardon me.”
Sunan at-Tirmidhi 3513, Sunan Ibn Majah 3850 (Sahih)

After Ramadan: Keep the Change Alive

The sign that your Ramadan was accepted is that you remain on the path afterwards. Choose two or three habits to keep: Fajr in the masjid or on time, a daily page of Quran, a weekly fast, a fixed charity or a daily morning adhkar. Keep them small enough to sustain.

The Prophet ﷺ encouraged fasting six days of Shawwal after Ramadan, which completes the reward of a full year. He also taught that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most regular ones. Eid is a time of joy, but it is also a time to turn the month's lessons into habits for the year.

Key takeawayA good Ramadan makes your normal days better.

“Whoever fasts Ramadan then follows it with six days of Shawwal, it is as if he fasted the whole year.”

Sahih Muslim 1164

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Turning Ramadan into a food festival instead of a month of worship.
  • Sleeping through the day and wasting the best hours.
  • Rushing the Quran without understanding or reflection.
  • Giving up after missing a day of goals instead of continuing.
  • Not planning what to keep after Eid.

Questions People Ask

What if I cannot fast because of illness or travel?Allah wants ease for you and not hardship. Make up the days later, or follow the ruling for those who cannot fast permanently. Ask a qualified scholar about your situation.
Is it better to finish the Quran quickly or read slowly with understanding?Both have value. A balanced approach is to complete at least one reading while giving time to understand some parts through translation or tafsir.
How can I keep the energy for the last ten nights?Conserve energy earlier by sleeping well, avoiding heavy meals and taking short rests during the day. Make the last ten nights your focus.
05

Practical Application: Start Today

Here is a seven-step Ramadan plan that works for workers, students, parents and busy leaders alike.

  1. Write your four goalsOne each for Quran, prayer, charity and character. Make them specific and realistic.
  2. Plan your Quran scheduleChoose how many pages after each prayer and set times. Keep the Quran where you can see it.
  3. Build your night routinePray tarawih and add a few rak'ahs of qiyam. Write your du'a list.
  4. Guard your tongue and screen timeSet a rule: no backbiting, no arguments, limited social media while fasting.
  5. Give dailySet a daily charity amount and feed at least one fasting person each week.
  6. Plan the last ten nightsArrange work and family to free your nights. Consider i'tikaf if possible.
  7. Choose what you will keepPick two or three habits to continue after Eid and plan the six days of Shawwal.

06

From My Leadership Journey

In my life of study and leadership I learned that the most successful seasons were those that began with a plan and ended with a review. Ramadan is the best season of the year for this approach. It comes with built-in structure: fixed times, fixed duties and a spiritual atmosphere that helps us.

As a leader I try to treat Ramadan as the time when I reset my character: my patience, my generosity and my attention to the people under my care. The leader who is changed in Ramadan changes the people around him in the months that follow.

07

Reflection Questions

  1. What is the one habit that would make my whole year better if Ramadan fixed it?
  2. How do I treat people when I am hungry and tired?
  3. Which night of Ramadan will I protect from distractions?
  4. How many pages of the Quran have I truly understood, not just read?
  5. What will I still be doing three months after Eid?
08

Conclusion and Call to Action

Ramadan is a gift that comes once a year, and no one knows if they will see another. Plan it, fill it with Quran, night prayer, charity and forgiveness, and carry its lessons into the months that follow.

Ask Allah to let you reach Ramadan, to help you fast and stand in it and to accept it from you. Then begin your plan, even today.

Your next stepWrite your four Ramadan goals today and share them with one family member or friend who will encourage you.
